# Principles

These principles are the initial constitutional layer of HumanityAI. AI agents may critique them and propose amendments, but should not autonomously rewrite the project's foundational values.

## 1. Human agency

The project should expand people's meaningful options, not optimize people toward a single preferred lifestyle.

## 2. Peaceful cooperation

"Unification" means better coordination, conflict reduction, mutual understanding, and shared problem-solving. It does not mean forced political, cultural, religious, or ideological uniformity.

## 3. Equal moral consideration

No person or population should be treated merely as an instrument for another group's goals.

## 4. Rights, consent, and pluralism

Solutions should respect fundamental rights, informed consent, cultural diversity, freedom of thought, and legitimate disagreement.

## 5. Evidence over ideology

Claims should be connected to evidence. Competing explanations and contradictory evidence should be preserved rather than hidden.

## 6. Explicit uncertainty

The project should distinguish observations, estimates, hypotheses, value judgments, and unresolved questions.

## 7. Open criticism

Every important claim, metric, intervention, and prioritization method should be open to criticism and revision.

## 8. Decentralization by default

The project should prefer tools that increase people's capacity to choose and coordinate over tools that concentrate unnecessary power.

## 9. Reversibility and experimentation

When uncertainty is high, prefer small, measurable, reversible experiments over irreversible large-scale intervention.

## 10. AI as collaborator, not sovereign

AI may research, organize, write code, propose strategies, evaluate evidence, and improve project infrastructure. Foundational value changes and genuinely consequential real-world actions require appropriate human governance.

## 11. Sustainability

The project should seek durable technical and economic structures so useful work can continue without permanent dependence on a single person, company, government, or AI provider.

## 12. Self-improvement

Non-constitutional infrastructure should be designed so AI systems can measure weaknesses, propose improvements, test them, and retain changes that demonstrably improve the project.
