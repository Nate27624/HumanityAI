# Reproduction note — I0014 OECD EU national AI strategies

Status: **reproduced with material qualification**

HumanityAI record checked: `I0014` in `data/indicators.json`.

## Claim checked

`I0014` records that **24 of 27 EU Member States had adopted a national AI strategy as of December 2024**, using the OECD's 2025 review of implementation of the EU Coordinated Plan on AI.

## Independent source check

Primary source: OECD, *Progress in Implementing the European Union Coordinated Plan on Artificial Intelligence (Volume 1)*, published 10 November 2025.

https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/progress-in-implementing-the-european-union-coordinated-plan-on-artificial-intelligence-volume-1_533c355d-en/full-report/overview_cd32064e.html

The OECD overview states directly that, **as of December 2024, 24 of the 27 EU Member States had adopted national AI strategies**, with the remaining three in the process of developing them. The detailed enabling-conditions chapter repeats the same 24/27 result and identifies Croatia, Greece, and the Slovak Republic as developing dedicated strategies at the report's analytical cut-off.

The report explains that its main evidence was collected through a survey of all EU Member States between July and October 2024, interviews between September and December 2024, country notes validated with the respective authorities, and desk research used to fill gaps. December 2024 is the report's analytical cut-off, although targeted 2025 updates were incorporated in places.

## Important temporal qualification

The 24/27 value is a **December 2024 snapshot**, not a current 2026 count. The OECD itself notes that Luxembourg launched a new national AI strategy in May 2025 but was not counted as an adopted strategy for the report's December-2024 analytical baseline. This illustrates why `I0014` should not be read as saying that exactly 24 EU states have strategies today.

## Construct qualification

The source also makes clear that adoption is much weaker evidence than effective governance. Implementation progress varies considerably, only 12 of 27 Member States had dedicated AI-strategy budgets with specific allocations in the reviewed material, and evaluation scope and transparency varied. The record therefore measures **formal strategy adoption**, not regulatory quality, implementation capacity, enforcement, public legitimacy, safety, rights protection, or outcomes.

## Result

**Reproduced with qualification.** The canonical value, denominator, geography, and December-2024 reference period are directly supported by the OECD source.

At least four interpretation risks should remain explicit:

1. **Snapshot risk:** 24/27 is tied to the December 2024 analytical cut-off and is not a live 2026 governance count.
2. **Adoption-versus-implementation:** publication of a strategy does not establish that its measures were funded, implemented, evaluated, or effective.
3. **Heterogeneity:** national strategies differ in scope, legal force, institutional ownership, budgets, monitoring, and substantive priorities.
4. **Regional scope:** the indicator covers EU Member States only and cannot support claims about global AI-governance readiness.
