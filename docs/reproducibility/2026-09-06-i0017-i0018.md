# Reproducibility check: I0017 and I0018

Date checked: 2026-09-06
Task: AT0004 — independently reproduce and challenge HumanityAI quantitative claims

This check uses the original cited sources where available and records newer authoritative context separately. No indicator value is changed merely because a later source uses different rounding or a newer vintage.

## I0017 — online digital identity access gap

**HumanityAI record:** `I0017`

**Recorded value:** 2.8 billion people without access to a government-recognized digital identity usable for online transactions in 2025.

**Original cited source:** World Bank, *Global progress in identification: 3 findings from the latest data* (2025-10-30), https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/digital-development/global-progress-in-identification--3-findings-from-the-latest-da

**Primary dataset page checked:** World Bank ID4D Global Dataset, https://id4d.worldbank.org/global-dataset

**Result:** **reproduced, with a newer-vintage qualification.**

The World Bank's 2025 ID4D material states that 2.8 billion people lacked access to digital ID systems facilitating online transactions. The same source also reports that, among surveyed adults living in countries where an online digital ID exists, about 46% reported owning one and 35% reported having used one. Those values match the HumanityAI record and its notes.

A newer World Bank Global Digital Public Infrastructure results page dated 2026-05-06 describes the gap as **2.9 billion** people. That does not make the historical `I0017` record false: the record is explicitly tied to the 2025 dataset/vintage and its cited source. The difference should be treated as evidence that this estimate is version-sensitive and subject to rounding or data revision, not as a reason to silently overwrite the older value.

**Interpretive qualification:** `I0017` should continue to be read as a 2025 access baseline, not as a timeless current count. Future updates should preserve source vintage and avoid comparing 2.8B and 2.9B as if they were independently measured contradictory populations without first checking methodology and revision notes.

## I0018 — OECD Truth Quest score

**HumanityAI record:** `I0018`

**Recorded value:** 60% average correct identification of the veracity of sampled online content across 21 countries.

**Original cited source:** OECD, *The OECD Truth Quest Survey: Methodology and findings* (2024-06-28), https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-oecd-truth-quest-survey_92a94c0f-en.html

**Corroborating OECD source checked:** *OECD Digital Economy Outlook 2024 (Volume 2)*, media consumption and privacy chapter, https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-digital-economy-outlook-2024-volume-2_3adf705b-en/full-report/media-consumption-and-privacy_31ab2233.html

**Result:** **reproduced.**

OECD reports 40,765 participants across 21 countries and states that respondents correctly identified the veracity of content 60% of the time on average. OECD also specifies that the headline average is a simple average of the 21 country scores, with post-stratification used within countries. This matches `I0018` and supports its existing limitation that the 60% figure is not a population-weighted global estimate.

The OECD material further confirms that performance depends on the content set: true claims were harder to identify on average than false or misleading claims. That supports HumanityAI's decision not to interpret the 60% headline score as a complete measure of real-world misinformation susceptibility or information integrity.

## Outcome

- `I0017`: **reproduced / qualified** — value is faithful to the cited 2025 World Bank source; a newer 2026 World Bank page uses 2.9B, so source vintage must remain explicit.
- `I0018`: **reproduced** — value, sample size, country count, and simple-average interpretation are supported by OECD primary material.
- No data correction is warranted from this check.
- The main reproducibility lesson is that authoritative global estimates can move across vintages even when the underlying construct is nominally the same; HumanityAI should preserve dated source versions rather than silently normalizing them.
