# P07 complementary baseline — informal employment in 2026

## Why this fills a real measurement gap

Existing P07 indicator `I0012` measures **labour-market access**: the ILO projects a 408 million global jobs gap in 2026 among people who want paid work but cannot access it. Its own limitations explicitly say that this does not measure job quality, earnings adequacy, bargaining power, productivity, underemployment intensity, or whether work expands human capability.

A complementary P07 baseline should therefore measure conditions **among people who are working**, not add another unemployment/access statistic.

## Proposed indicator

- **Concept:** workers in informal employment
- **Value:** 2.1 billion workers
- **Value type:** projection / current 2026 ILO estimate
- **Reference period:** 2026
- **Geography:** World
- **Publisher:** International Labour Organization
- **Primary source:** *Employment and Social Trends 2026*
- **Primary URL:** https://www.ilo.org/publications/flagship-reports/employment-and-social-trends-2026
- **Same-publisher cross-check:** *Global job quality stagnates despite resilient growth*
- **Cross-check URL:** https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/global-job-quality-stagnates-despite-resilient-growth
- **Publication date:** 14 January 2026
- **Direct vs derived:** directly reported by the ILO; no HumanityAI arithmetic is needed for the 2.1 billion figure.

The ILO's 2026 flagship material reports that the global jobs gap is projected at 408 million while, separately, about **2.1 billion workers are expected to be in informal employment**. It frames informality as a job-quality deficit often associated with limited access to social protection, rights at work, and job security. The same material reports nearly 300 million workers living in extreme working poverty, reinforcing that stable unemployment does not imply adequate job quality.

## Why this is complementary rather than duplicate

`I0012` asks roughly: **how many people want paid work but cannot access it?**

This proposed indicator asks: **among people who do have work, how widespread is employment outside formal arrangements and protections?**

The populations overlap conceptually with other labour-market vulnerabilities but the constructs are different. The 2.1 billion informal workers must not be added to the 408 million jobs gap, unemployment, working-poverty, underemployment, or other labour-market counts to create a pseudo-total.

## Limitations and interpretation

1. **Informality is not synonymous with bad work.** Informal employment is heterogeneous across countries, sectors, occupations, and worker preferences. Some informal arrangements may provide flexibility or income opportunities; the measure should not be treated as a direct welfare-loss estimate.
2. **The number is a projection/estimate, not a census.** Cross-country labour statistics depend on national surveys and harmonized ILO estimation procedures, with uncertainty and reporting lags.
3. **It is a structural quality/protection indicator, not an outcome score.** It does not directly measure wages, hours, safety, bargaining power, productivity, autonomy, satisfaction, or capability expansion.
4. **Country context matters.** Legal definitions, enforcement, social-protection systems, and the meaning of informality differ across economies.
5. **Do not add overlapping populations.** Informal workers can also be in working poverty, underemployed, young, female, migrant, or otherwise disadvantaged. These dimensions should remain separate.
6. **Absolute count and rate answer different questions.** A 2.1 billion count communicates scale, but longitudinal diagnosis should also inspect the informal-employment share because global population and employment growth can raise counts even when rates improve.

## Recommendation

This is a stronger next P07 baseline than another unemployment statistic because it directly fills the job-quality gap already identified in `I0012`. A later canonical machine-readable record should preserve projection status and the non-additivity warning above. A useful additional companion metric would be an ILO informal-employment **rate** or working-poverty rate with a clearly aligned year and denominator, rather than collapsing them into one composite score.

## Disposition

**Recommended for canonicalization** as a separate P07 indicator after normal repository review/validation. No claim is made here that informality is equivalent to poor outcomes or that reducing informality is always beneficial independent of institutional context.
