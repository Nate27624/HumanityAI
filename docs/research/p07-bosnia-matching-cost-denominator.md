Worker D assigned a bounded P07 information-value task: recover a source-grounded incremental cost for the Bosnia and Herzegovina private job-matching intervention used in the canonical youth-ALMP routing framework. If no defensible comparable cost existed, the instruction was to record that negative result rather than invent a denominator.

## Result

The missing **contract payment schedule is recoverable**, but the missing **economic resource cost of matching is not**.

The World Bank implementation-completion documentation for the same Bosnia and Herzegovina Provision of Private Employment Services project reports the first-phase performance-based schedule as:

- **USD 90 per jobseeker** meeting the counseling/job-search-assistance condition (profiling, assessment, and Employment Action Plan), derived from USD 225,000 / 2,500 beneficiaries; and
- **USD 1,100 per verified formal placement** under intermediation, derived from the initial first-phase target of 550 verified placements and USD 605,000 allocated.

The experiment randomized jobseekers between counseling/IAP services and the same package plus access to matching. The final 2024 World Bank Policy Research Working Paper reports an intent-to-treat effect of additional matching of **+5.8 percentage points** in formal employment at 3–6 months and **+4.1 percentage points** at 21–24 months. A related World Bank policy-note version reports slightly different rounded/specification figures (6.4 pp and 4.2 pp); for derived quantities below, this note uses the final WPS estimates already used in HumanityAI's canonical P07 comparison rather than silently mixing versions.

This means the contract schedule supports a narrow derived quantity:

- short-run incremental **outcome-contingent payment** implied by the final-WPS ITT employment difference: `0.058 × $1,100 = $63.80 per treatment-assigned jobseeker`;
- medium-run mechanical equivalent using the later employment difference: `0.041 × $1,100 = $45.10 per treatment-assigned jobseeker`.

The short-run quantity is the more directly aligned fiscal interpretation because the contract's placement payment required verified formal employment for at least three consecutive months. The medium-run figure is retained only as a sensitivity-style mechanical application of the same payment schedule, not as evidence that the provider received a second or continuing payment at 21–24 months.

These are **not program resource-cost estimates** and must not be used as portable cost-effectiveness denominators.

## Why the recovered denominator is not a true incremental matching cost

1. The USD 1,100 is an **outcome payment for a verified formal placement**, not an invoice for the marginal staff time, employer outreach, screening, introductions, monitoring, or administrative effort used to provide matching.
2. The experimental paper states that the provider received the placement payment when a beneficiary obtained formal employment; matching was a tool available to the provider rather than a separately priced service. Therefore, the payment schedule does not isolate the resource cost of general or specific matching.
3. Both randomized groups received the counseling/IAP service, so the USD 90 base payment is not the incremental treatment cost of matching.
4. The later World Bank completion report explicitly says the provider **spent more funds than it could get back**, because expenses could not be claimed when performance conditions were not achieved. That is direct evidence that disbursement is not equal to economic resource cost.
5. The completion report identifies sizeable contracting/monitoring transaction costs and says the performance-based instrument was difficult for the provider, including that some PBC values/incentives needed redesign. This further weakens any interpretation of the USD 1,100 as a market/resource-cost measure.
6. The 2025 completion report covers the broader multi-phase P171433 operation, while the randomized evaluation was implemented during the first-phase calls in 2021–2022. The initial PBC 2a/3a schedule is therefore the relevant schedule to verify against the evaluated phase; later PBC 2b/3b/3c values should not be substituted into the experiment without a phase mapping.

## Decision delta

**The Bosnia gap is now narrower but not closed.** We can replace “no cost information” with a more precise statement:

> The first-phase contract encoded roughly USD 90 per counseling/IAP case plus USD 1,100 per verified formal placement. Applying the final WPS matching ITT yields about USD 64 per assigned participant in additional short-run outcome-contingent placement payments. This is a fiscal/incentive denominator, not the true incremental resource cost of matching, which remains unobserved.

This changes P07 routing in four ways:

- **START** distinguishing fiscal/outcome-contingent payment from economic resource cost in employment-service comparisons.
- **STOP** treating “Bosnia cost missing” as a completely blank field; the first-phase contract incentive schedule is known.
- **CONTINUE TO BLOCK** any scalar Bosnia-vs-entrepreneurship cost-effectiveness ranking until staff/operating/contracting resource cost or a defensible total incremental implementation cost is recovered.
- **MORE** attention to incentive design: the evidence implies that the USD 1,100 success payment shaped provider effort and cream-skimming, while later implementation evidence shows the performance-based model could leave provider expenditures unrecovered and may require tiering for harder-to-place clients.

Confidence: **high** on the USD 90 / USD 1,100 first-phase payment schedule and on the payment-versus-resource-cost distinction; **moderate-high** on the $63.80 short-run derived payment quantity because it mechanically combines the final WPS ITT with the first-phase placement payment, subject to Worker C confirming phase correspondence; **low** on any attempt to infer true incremental economic cost from these payments.
