## Decision delta

**Bosnia remains non-rankable on cost-effectiveness with the currently public evidence reviewed here.** The primary study exposes the payment *structure* but not the payment *amounts*: the provider was paid for each Individual Action Plan and received an additional payment for each beneficiary in formal employment for at least three consecutive months. The paper also documents substantial contracting/monitoring costs and explicitly warns that scale-up may have limited cost-effectiveness, but it does not publish the fee schedule or a separable incremental matching cost.

A focused search of the study, its World Bank record, grant identifier, and web-indexed procurement/program material did not expose a contract payment schedule. Therefore no Bosnia cost-per-assigned-participant, cost-per-specific-match, or cost-per-additional-formal-job value is added. **Do not infer one from total program/grant budgets or from outcome-contingent payment language.**

The Benin follow-up materially improves the next comparison. The final World Bank impact-evaluation report separates three randomized arms and follows outcomes to **45 months after training / 38 months after the grant**. Training-only effects were sustained and grew over time; at the final follow-up total monthly earnings were higher by about **US$15.78 for men and US$9.05 for women** relative to control. The report's broader summary rounds the sustained monthly earnings gains to **US$29 for men and US$16 for women** when discussing the trajectory/cost calculation; this discrepancy reflects different reported summaries/specifications and should be preserved rather than silently harmonized.

The report gives fully loaded component costs of about **US$1,050 per training beneficiary** and **US$629 per grant beneficiary**. Its own back-of-the-envelope calculation says the training cost is recouped through earnings after roughly **36 months for men** and **64 months for women**, with the women's estimate explicitly assuming post-follow-up persistence. Grant-only and training+grant arms do not show a robust earnings/business-performance advantage; the report states that the grant tended to have null or negative primary business/earnings effects and could cancel training gains when combined.

### Allocation consequence

For the current P07 routing framework:

1. **Bosnia private matching:** keep as an efficacy/persistence signal, especially for youth/lower-skill jobseekers, but block scalar cost-effectiveness ranking until the contract fee schedule or a defensible incremental resource-cost denominator is found.
2. **Benin training-only:** promote as the cleaner cost/persistence comparator within the entrepreneurship-support family because it has a defined per-capita cost, three follow-ups, and sustained arm-specific earnings effects.
3. **Benin grant-only / training+grant:** do not assume added capital improves the training package. The final evaluation instead supplies a direct STOP signal for treating more expensive bundles as automatically better.
4. **Cross-study ranking:** still blocked. Bosnia's missing incremental cost and different wage-employment outcome remain denominator-incompatible with Benin's cost-per-beneficiary and self-employment earnings outcomes.

Confidence: **high** that the public Bosnia evidence reviewed here does not expose a defensible incremental cost denominator; **high** on Benin follow-up timing and component cost definitions; **moderate-high** on the allocation consequence because cross-context portability remains limited.

## Bosnia: bounded negative search result

Primary paper: Balavac-Orlic, Giles, Hari & Ovadiya (2024), World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 10826.

What is directly supported:

- 1,541 jobseekers were randomized: 765 to counseling + matching and 776 to counseling only.
- The provider received a payment per completed Individual Action Plan plus an additional payment for a beneficiary securing formal employment that persisted for at least three consecutive months.
- The contract targeted 500 sustained employments.
- Only about 23% of the matching arm received the intensive specific-matching service.
- The paper explicitly says matching is costly to the provider and notes sizeable contracting-out transaction costs for the public authority.
- The paper recommends a tiered payment scheme if policy makers want to reduce cream-skimming of less-employable jobseekers.

What is **not** exposed in the reviewed public material:

- payment per IAP;
- outcome payment per sustained employment;
- total provider contract value attributable to the evaluated service;
- incremental cost of counseling + matching versus counseling only;
- realized cost per assigned participant or per specific match.

This absence is decision-relevant. Using a broad World Bank/SIDA grant amount, a project budget, or an unrelated public-employment-service program budget as the denominator would mix research, administration, base counseling, matching, monitoring, and possibly other activities and would create false precision.

Sources checked:

- World Bank WPS 10826: https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/099503006262434572/private-provisioning-of-employment-services-experimental-evidence-from-bosnia-and-herzegovina
- DOI record: https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-10826
- World Bank grant identifier referenced by the study record: TF0A6954, "Developing Private Employment Services in Bosnia and Herzegovina"

## Benin: arm-specific persistence and cost extraction

Primary source: *Final evaluation report of Benin's Youth Employment Project*, World Bank, June 2022.

Source URL: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099005006302210770/pdf/P1477800853c6e050ba2403b0a1ba2239d.pdf

### Experimental arms

- **T1:** life/business-skills training + cash grant
- **T2:** training only
- **T3:** cash grant only
- **Control:** neither intervention

The evaluation included 3,444 youth and three follow-up rounds. Timing was approximately:

| Follow-up | After training | After grant |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 15 months | 8 months |
| 2 | 27 months | 20 months |
| 3 | 45 months | 38 months |

### Final-follow-up decision-bearing results

At follow-up 3, the training-only arm had the clearest positive business-performance and earnings signal. Table 5 reports:

| Arm | Men: monthly earnings effect | Women: monthly earnings effect | Primary interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Training + grant | +US$15.30 | +US$3.11 | mixed; no robust advantage over training-only |
| Training only | +US$15.78 | +US$9.05 | positive and statistically significant at final follow-up |
| Grant only | +US$0.72 | +US$0.47 | essentially null on earnings |

The report's narrative summary also states sustained monthly earnings increases of about **US$29 for men** and **US$16 for women** for training-only. Because the report presents both those narrative figures and the Table 5 follow-up-3 coefficients, future modeling should identify which specification/summary is being used rather than combining them.

### Cost definition

The evaluation reports:

- training cost: **US$1,050 per capita**;
- cash-grant component cost: **US$629 per capita**.

These are not transfer face values alone. The report says the per-capita calculation includes prorated project management, sensitization/communications, enrollment, lotteries, monitoring/supervision by local employment agents, training design/trainer preparation/delivery, grant amounts, and payment fees.

The evaluation's own simple earnings-based recoupment calculation is:

- men: training cost recouped after about **36 months**;
- women: about **64 months**, conditional on effects continuing after the final follow-up.

This is a useful source-grounded cost/persistence field, but it is not a complete welfare or fiscal cost-benefit analysis: it does not monetize spillovers, risk, taxes, business survival, general equilibrium effects, or alternative uses of participant time.
