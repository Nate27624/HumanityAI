A later audited project financial statement does expose large project-level financial amounts, including a Performance Based Condition for “Increasing job placements through intermediation,” but these figures are **not the randomized-arm incremental cost** and should not be divided by the evaluation sample or treatment effect.

Therefore the Bosnia row in the canonical P07 routing framework should remain **cost-incomplete for scalar cost-effectiveness comparisons**. ## What the primary sources establish

### Evaluation-period service and payment architecture

The World Bank policy note reports that 4,209 applications produced 2,592 eligible jobseekers and 1,606 profiled beneficiaries. Participants received profiling, an Individual Action Plan (IAP), counselling/job-search assistance, and—depending on randomized assignment and subsequent provider choices—general or specific job matching.

The private provider was paid for each IAP developed and received an additional performance payment for each beneficiary in formal employment for at least three consecutive months. The source does **not** report the amount of either payment. The evaluation is explicitly about the added effect of access to costly matching relative to counselling, but it does not publish an incremental resource-cost schedule for that randomized contrast.

Primary source:
- World Bank, *Policy Note: Private Provisioning of Employment Services in Bosnia and Herzegovina* (Balavac-Orlic, Giles, Hari, Ovadiya), project/evaluation documentation: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099061424085014380/pdf/P1668481ccc5d707183ad1b71222e10572.pdf

### Audited project financials are real but denominator-incompatible

Audited financial statements for the broader Provision of Private Employment Services project report:

- total amended grant: **USD 2,673,717** after partial cancellation;
- Performance Based Condition #2, “Increasing job placement through counselling and job search assistance”: **USD 540,000** budget allocation;
- Performance Based Condition #3, “Increasing job placements through intermediation”: **USD 1,650,000** budget allocation;
- total project expenditures through 31 May 2025: **USD 2,212,782**;
- project expenditure categories include staff, facilities, office costs, training/stipends, and intermediation-related expenditure.

These amounts cover the wider project and multiple years/activities. They are not a clean treatment-arm accounting decomposition for the 2021–2022 randomized evaluation. The financial statements also state that the grant agreement defines eligible expenditure categories and a maximum grant amount, while disbursements are tied to achievement of Performance Based Conditions. That accounting structure does not identify the marginal cost caused by assignment to matching rather than counselling.

Primary source:
- Alma Career BH / World Bank, *Provision of private employment services (PPES): Financial Statements for the period from 1 January 2024 to 31 May 2025*, independent auditor report: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099072925092028305/pdf/P171433-a8cb6e3a-3ef3-42a9-86c5-77ac8a0371a8.pdf

## Why plausible calculations would be invalid

Do **not** compute a Bosnia cost-per-participant or cost-per-additional-job by dividing the USD 1.65 million PBC#3 allocation, total grant, or total expenditure by the randomized treatment sample, profiled beneficiaries, or estimated additional jobs. That would mix:

1. different implementation periods;
2. project-wide intermediation outputs with the evaluation's counselling-vs-matching contrast;
3. fixed/shared staff and operating costs with marginal matching effort;
4. later project expansions and Performance Based Conditions with the original randomized cohort;
5. reimbursement/budget amounts with economic resource cost.

Such a ratio could look precise while answering the wrong causal cost question.

## Bounded search result

Focused search checked the 2024 World Bank evaluation/policy-note materials, the project identification and grant trail for P171433, and the later audited project financial statements. The search recovered the payment mechanism and project-level financial envelopes but **not** the contract/payment schedule needed to isolate:

- payment per IAP;
- payment per verified sustained placement;
- incremental provider labor/operating cost for matching relative to counselling;
- realized cost per randomized matching assignment;
- realized cost per additional formal job attributable to assignment to matching.

This is a useful negative result because it prevents the newly canonical P07 routing framework from drifting into a false scalar ranking based on a project-budget numerator.
