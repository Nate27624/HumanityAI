## Decision delta

A focused search did **not** recover a defensible incremental per-participant cost for the exact randomized counseling-plus-matching treatment evaluated by Balavac-Orlic et al. (2024). The cost field in the canonical P07 study table should therefore remain unresolved for scalar cost-effectiveness comparisons.

A later World Bank implementation-completion report for the related Bosnia and Herzegovina Provision of Private Employment Services project (P171433) does expose performance-based payment amounts, including **US$1,100 per qualifying formal job placement** under PBC #3b and #3c. That amount is useful as an implementation/payment-schedule anchor, but it is **not a defensible estimate of the incremental cost of assignment to active matching in the randomized evaluation**. It is an outcome-contingent provider payment from a later, broader project package and uses a different denominator.

**Result:** keep Bosnia out of any cross-study scalar cost-effectiveness ranking until a source tied to the experimental intervention exposes counseling base payment, incremental matching effort/payment, or realized treatment-arm delivery cost on an assignment-compatible denominator.

Confidence: **high** that US$1,100 must not be substituted for the missing experimental treatment cost; **moderate-high** that the currently public/located sources do not expose the required denominator.

## What the experimental paper establishes

The World Bank working paper evaluates outsourcing employment services to a private provider. Participants received profiling/counseling, while the matching arm could additionally receive active employer matching. The provider was paid for each individual action plan and received an additional payment when a beneficiary secured verified formal employment. The paper explains the payment structure and independent employment verification but, in the located public text, does not state a standardized incremental price for assignment to active matching that can be compared with the per-assigned-participant costs used for Benin or Uganda in PR #87.

Source:
- Balavac-Orlic, Merima; Giles, John T.; Hari, Siddharth; Ovadiya, Mirey. 2024. *Private Provisioning of Employment Services: Experimental Evidence from Bosnia and Herzegovina*. World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 10826. https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099503006262434572/pdf/IDU1ec3500041edc51441119ce5169b762149bd2.pdf

Relevant payment description: payment for every individual action plan developed after profiling plus an additional payment for each beneficiary who obtained verified formal employment. This architecture creates an outcome incentive but does not by itself identify the marginal resource cost of the randomized matching arm.

## Why US$1,100 is not the missing PR #87 cost

The US$1,100 figure should **not** be entered as "Bosnia matching cost per participant" because:

1. **Outcome denominator mismatch.** It is paid per qualifying placed person, not per participant assigned to matching.
2. **Incremental-treatment mismatch.** It does not isolate the resource difference between counseling-only and counseling-plus-active-matching treatment arms.
3. **Program-vintage/scope mismatch.** The completion report covers a broader 2020-2025 private-employment-services project with multiple services and restructurings; the experimental paper's estimand is the causal increment from active matching relative to counseling.
4. **Cost versus transfer/payment distinction.** A performance payment is a contractual price/incentive, not necessarily the provider's economic resource cost.
5. **Selection/endogeneity.** Outcome-contingent payments depend on realized placements and therefore cannot be treated as an ex ante per-assigned cost without additional arm-level service-use and payment data.

## Safe inference and current decision rule

The later US$1,100 payment makes one useful point: a real Bosnia implementation used substantial outcome-contingent placement incentives rather than costless referral. It does **not** resolve whether Bosnia-style active matching is more cost-effective than entrepreneurship support.

For P07 allocation:

- **KEEP** the binding-constraint routing framework from PR #87.
- **KEEP** Bosnia's cost field unresolved for scalar use.
- **DO NOT** convert US$1,100 per verified placement into a per-assigned-participant cost or compare it directly with Jordan, Benin, or Uganda.
- **NEXT:** move to the controller's fallback gap: Benin arm-specific persistence/cost-effectiveness, unless a primary contract/POM tied to the randomized Bosnia treatment later exposes arm-compatible payment or delivery-cost data.
