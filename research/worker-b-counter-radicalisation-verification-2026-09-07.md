# Worker B independent verification: counter-radicalisation intervention candidates

Date: 2026-09-07

Scope: independent source verification of `IC0005` (case-management evidence gap) and `IC0006` (targeted counter-narratives). This note does not promote either candidate into the canonical machine-readable evidence registry and does not recommend deployment of counter-radicalisation programmes.

## Result

Both candidates are directionally and numerically supported by the cited Campbell systematic-review sources. The most decision-relevant conclusion is not that either intervention class is effective or ineffective, but that the available evidence is substantially weaker on consequential violent-radicalisation outcomes than programme adoption or intuitive plausibility might suggest.

### IC0005 — case management

The 2024 Campbell review reports no eligible studies for its effectiveness objective in countering radicalisation to violence. It identified 47 studies relevant to implementation questions and eight systematic reviews concerning case-management tools or approaches for other forms of violence. Therefore `IC0005` is appropriately labelled `insufficient_evidence`: zero eligible effectiveness evaluations is an evidence gap, not evidence of a null effect.

The candidate's caution about implementation evidence is also warranted. The review explicitly separates implementation questions from effectiveness and states that no conclusions about effectiveness can be drawn for counter-radicalisation case management.

### IC0006 — targeted counter-narratives

The 2020 Campbell review includes 19 independent studies, including 12 randomized controlled trials, with studies mainly involving university or high-school populations and with North America the most represented region. It reports heterogeneous results: some counter-stereotypical exemplar, alternative-narrative, and inoculation approaches improved selected secondary risk-factor outcomes, while persuasion did not show a significant effect and primary-outcome evidence such as intent to act violently was sparse/inconclusive.

The candidate's wording — that the review does not establish an effect on intent to act violently — is preferable to interpreting the review's plain-language headline literally as proof of a zero effect. The detailed review text characterizes primary-outcome evidence as sparse and inconclusive, so uncertainty rather than a demonstrated null is the safer synthesis.

## Newer evidence-landscape check

A 2024 update on Campbell's Countering Violent Extremism programme describes an evidence-and-gap map based on nearly 70,000 screened records, yielding 67 eligible impact-evaluation studies (14 randomized trials and 51 quasi-experimental studies, plus two systematic reviews). It reports that evidence was concentrated in policing and police-inclusive multi-agency interventions, that major geographic gaps remained, and that very limited research assessed effectiveness using violent-extremism and/or radicalisation-to-violence outcomes.

This newer landscape evidence does not directly update the effect estimate for targeted counter-narratives and does not overturn either candidate. It does strengthen the broader qualification that the field's growing evaluation count should not be mistaken for a mature evidence base on the outcomes of greatest interest.

## Epistemic qualifications

- The Campbell sources are systematic reviews, but they are not independent replications of one another and arise partly from the same broader Campbell countering-violent-extremism research programme.
- `IC0005` should not be converted from `insufficient_evidence` to `ineffective` without eligible outcome evidence.
- `IC0006` should preserve proxy-versus-outcome separation. Changes in threat perception, in-group favouritism, or out-group hostility are not equivalent to reductions in violent intent or observed violence.
- External validity is limited by geography, participant populations, intervention heterogeneity, ideological context, outcome definitions, and the age of the counter-narrative search (through August 2018).
- Effectiveness evidence alone would not resolve civil-liberties, privacy, discrimination, legitimacy, free-expression, or targeting risks.
- Neither candidate establishes cost-effectiveness, implementation scalability, or HumanityAI comparative advantage.
